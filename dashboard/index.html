Learned so far

  • Recency beats seasonality here. Seasonal naive (VN1 1.02) loses badly to last-week naive (0.58): heavy product lifecycle churn makes the year-ago week a poor reference for most series.
  • Dead-series zeroing was a no-op. MA13 with 26-week dead-zeroing scored identically to plain MA13 — a 13-week window already forecasts 0 for anything dead longer than the window.
  • The sparse tail is unsolved. Every baseline scores ≈2.0 (worst possible) on "insufficient" series — but they carry only 3.9% of volume. The big open segments are lumpy (VN1 ≈ 0.70, 20% of volume) and erratic.
  • The VN1 score is not segment-separable. Cycle 1's router improved the smooth segment exactly as predicted (0.51→0.39) and still lost: that segment's over-forecast had been offsetting the portfolio's under-forecast, so removing it deepened net bias and the global bias term ate the WAPE gain. Segment gains don't add up.
  • Client-level holiday re-anchoring refuted. Scaling naive by client week-of-year indices made bias worse (−5%→−15%): lifecycle churn contaminates calendar indices. The holiday blind spot stays open — seasonality must live inside the demand model, not as post-hoc scaling.
  • Trailing debias helps steady regimes, hurts at seasonal turns. The debiased router (0.5642) cut portfolio bias to −2.6% but scaled up into the post-holiday January window (+29% bias there) and failed the origin-majority rule.
  • First gate PASS: the global LightGBM (0.5622). Wins 3 of 4 origins with near-zero bias (−0.8%) and no segment regressions. Its remaining weakness is shared by every model: the January origin — its 24-week training window has never seen a post-holiday decline. Training coverage of a full seasonal cycle is the registered next lever.
  • Zero-shot foundation models lose overall but win the edges. Chronos-Bolt (trimmed-mean readout, 0.5396) and TimesFM (0.6512) never beat the GBM pooled, yet they are the best models in the ledger on smooth series and at the January turn (Chronos Jan origin 0.730 vs GBM 0.892) — with no training. Their weakness is a fixable readout bias, not accuracy: they over-forecast lumpy (Chronos-qmean +15.2%).
  • Architecture beat the deep-net loss story. Same Poisson loss, same data: N-HiTS (0.5677) nearly matches the champion while PatchTST (0.6516) trails badly — the backbone, not the count likelihood, decided it. Covariates (price, calendar, age) would not integrate stably at the untuned config, so their information value stays unresolved here.
  • The blend is the new champion (0.5192) — Stage 3’s bet paid off. A pinned 50/50 average of the 56-anchor GBM (lumpy bias −10.5%) and Chronos-qmean (lumpy bias +15.2%) cancels the lumpy bias to +2.3% — the first challenger ever to clear the lumpy segment gate, and it improves that class (0.678 vs 0.682) rather than merely surviving it. It wins all 4 origins, fixes January (0.892→0.564), and is better than the old champion on every ≥10%-volume segment; paired bootstrap p = 0.000. Different families fail in different places, so combining them wins — ROADMAP prediction 3 confirmed.
